Semiconductor

A semiconductor is a material that is made of silicon or germanium or gallium arsenide, whose electrical conductivity falls between conductor and insulator . Conductor like copper and Insulator  like glass. It controls conductivity by allowing and restricting current flow by using external factors such as voltage, temperature. Semiconductors are very essential for us. It is playing an important role in the rapid growth of technology. For instance smartphones, laptops , tractors and satellites and missiles etc. We can cen change their conductivity by  varying the temperature and adding impurities through doping, by creating p - type semiconductor with positive holes  and n- type semiconductor with extra electrons, by which we made electronics devices such as chips , diodes, and transistors. which is most helpful in the growth of technology.

The global trade history took a significant turning point through the decline of the British East India Company and Dutch East India Company. These were once dominant forces in the cotton and spice trade, which held together networks across Asia and Europe as powerful monopolies. But the decline started in the 18th and early 19th centuries, the reason being a combination of economic, political and administrative challenges led to their downfall. One major factor was the Dutch VOC suffered from the mountain of debts, inefficient bureaucracy, and receding profits. The increasing corruption among the officials weakened administrative efficiency and there was a misuse of resources and lack of accountability. This was when the other European traders and private merchants entered the Asian markets. Thus, the demand and supply patterns shifted, which led to the reduction of the company’s monopoly advantage. The Industrial revolution completely weakened the exclusive trading rights and promoted the growth of free trade policies. This mechanized production in Europe reduced the dependency on traditional trade systems this way. This led to the government taking over the direct control over trade and colonies. The East India Company lost their credibility due to their exploitative governance in India. The VOC was officially dissolved in 1799 due to bankruptcy. And the revolt of 1857 exposed the East India Company’s administrative failures. The British crown finally took direct control in 1858, ending the company’s rule. &#xA;
