British & Dutch Cotton Trade Routes & Logistics

Whenever we talk about cotton. We also want to know about its trade  routes and logistics. At the end of 16th century and starting years of 17th century, major transformation happened. It was the time of the start of the cotton trade. The cotton trade routes formed one of the important trade networks in the world in between 17th and 19th century. And it had become the backbone of the early world economy. By transportation, trade and production, some continents linked with each other.

Historically, India was center of trade routes. Through silk route and ocean paths , India’s cotton trade routes connected to east and west. That means it connected India to South East Asia, China and Europe. Over the century, this route was important as silk route. Through that India especially bengal and Coronmandal coast exported cotton fibre across the world. From the first century, India had started exporting cotton across the world, where Tagara (Ter of Maharastra) was the chief center. In the ancient time, this routes went china from India through Central Asia and South East Asia. And Ocean trade route was enough active for South East Asia from Bay of Bangal and Coromandel coast. This route form triangular shape. This route started from Britain , brought workers from west Africa and went the cotton gardend of America. and From there return back Britain with row cotton.

For transportation , there was two European powerful trade, the first one was British which managed by The British East India company which was founded on December 31, 1600, by royal from queen Elizabeth I and second one wasDutch which managed by The Dutch East India Company, which was found on 20 March 1602. Through a complex trading network, this trade routes connected the production regions of Asia, Africa and the Americas to Europe’s industrial centeres and global markets. They have a system which included ships,warehouse, ports and financial institutions and labor .

_The British East India Company _ During the 17th and 18th century , by controlling trade routes, The British East India Company played a crucial role in changing economics condition.In starting time they exported finished cotton from India to Europe. where its demand was realy high. From late 18th century, they started to exporting row cotton insteed of finished cotton. This cotton was sent to Canton in China for tea exchange.Gujarat was major cotton producing area and other are Maharastra, Telangana and Andhra Pradesh, which supplied cotton through Bombay and Surat Ports. And another port was Awadh via Culcutta to China. In 19th century, From India row cotton transported to Britain.After that they manufactured textile and export again in lot of country such as India, China and Africa. Main ports was Madras, Bombay and Culcutta.
